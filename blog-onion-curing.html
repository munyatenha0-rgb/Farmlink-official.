Curing and storing onions through the rainy season

6 August 2026 · FarmLink Team · 4 min read

A well-cured bucket of onions is one of the hardest-working purchases a Harare kitchen can make — it quietly seasons meals for months. A poorly stored one becomes a bucket of disappointment by October. The difference is curing, and it starts before the onions reach you.

What curing means

Curing is letting the outer skins dry completely so the onion seals itself. Our partner farms field-cure for several days after harvest — which is why Ncube Gardens onions arrive with dry, papery skins rather than moist ones. If your onions ever feel damp, spread them in the sun for a day before storing.

Braid or ventilate

Traditional braiding is not decoration — hanging onions gives airflow on all sides and keeps them off damp floors. If you store in buckets or crates, drill extra holes, keep them off the ground on a pallet or bricks, and never seal the lid.

Where to keep them

Cool, dry, dark, and airy: a pantry shelf, under-counter space away from the sink, or a shaded spare room. Not the fridge (humidity rots them), not next to potatoes (both spoil faster together), and not against an outside wall that sweats in the rains.

The monthly check

Once a month, tip the bucket out and inspect. One rotting onion gives off moisture that takes the rest. Soft or sprouting bulbs go to the pot first — sprouted onions are still perfectly good cooked. Kept this way, a bucket bought in June is still seasoning stews in November.
