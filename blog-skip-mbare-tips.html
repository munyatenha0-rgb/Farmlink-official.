Five ways Harare households save on fresh produce

25 June 2026 · FarmLink Team · 4 min read

Fresh food in Harare is rarely expensive because of the farmer. It is expensive because of the middle layers between the farm gate and your kitchen — transport, market fees, spoilage, and everyone's margin on top. Here are five ways buying direct changes the maths.

1. Buy at farm-gate prices

A crate that leaves Mazowe at $13 often resells in the city at $20 or more. On FarmLink you pay the listed farm price plus a flat $3 checkpoint fee — see exactly how it adds up on the pricing page.

2. Split crates with neighbours

A full crate of tomatoes is a lot for one household — but split four ways with your complex or street, each family pays a few dollars for produce that lasts the week. The $3 checkpoint fee splits too.

3. Order before 10am

Orders placed before 10am join the same day's collection route. Later orders wait an extra day — fine for planning, wasteful if you needed tonight's dinner ingredients. Check route days for your district.

4. Buy what is in season on the farm

When a farm is harvesting heavily, its listed prices are at their lowest and quality is at its peak. The market page shows harvest dates for a reason — "harvested today" listings are the best value on the site.

5. Waste less, save more

Most household produce budgets lose money to spoilage, not price. Our storage guides — tomatoes and onions — routinely stretch a crate from four days to two weeks. That alone can cut a produce budget by a third.
