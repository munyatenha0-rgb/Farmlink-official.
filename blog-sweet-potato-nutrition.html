Why orange sweet potatoes belong on every plate

9 July 2026 · FarmLink Team · 3 min read

The orange flesh in Tshuma Fields sweet potatoes is beta-carotene — the plant form of vitamin A — and it is the single biggest nutritional difference between orange and white varieties.

Vitamin A where it matters

Vitamin A supports night vision, skin and the immune system. Deficiency remains common in the region, especially in children — and one medium orange sweet potato can cover a full day's need. It is one reason nutrition programmes across southern Africa promote exactly this crop.

Slow energy, steady fullness

Sweet potatoes digest slowly compared to many starches, which means steadier energy and longer fullness — useful for long school and work days. Boiled or roasted, they need no added fat to be filling.

A crop that survives the hard months

Sweet potatoes tolerate dry spells that punish maize, and they store for weeks in a cool, dark corner without refrigeration. For farms like Tshuma Fields in Bindura, that hardiness means a crop that sells reliably — and for your kitchen, it means food security on a budget.

Three ways to eat more of them

Boil and eat plain with salt as a snack; cube into stews in place of some potato; or roast wedges in a hot oven until caramelised at the edges. A $6 bucket goes further than most people expect.
